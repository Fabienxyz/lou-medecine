# Learning Profile Extraction

## Executive Summary

- Lou studies through QCM correction and focuses on discordant answers.
- She tries to understand why an answer is true or false, not just memorize it.
- She is sensitive to wording differences: “possible sign” vs “typical sign.”
- She builds comparison rules between close clinical situations.
- She asks for mechanism-based explanations when confused.
- She uses screenshots of QCM corrections as the starting point.
- She validates understanding by challenging apparent inconsistencies across QCMs.
- She benefits from concise rule tables and contrastive explanations.
- She struggles when the same sign changes status depending on clinical context.
- She needs explicit distinction between “trigger,” “direct source,” and “associated consequence.”

## 1. Learning Workflow

1. Lou starts from a QCM correction screenshot.
2. She identifies the discordant item.
3. She asks why the expected answer differs from her answer.
4. She compares the current QCM with a previous QCM.
5. When an inconsistency appears, she asks for the underlying rule.
6. She seems to move toward memorization only after the logic is clarified.

Evidence:
- “expliques”
- “Je ne comprends pas parce que parfois les maturités sont considérées comme un signe normal, parfois non.”
- “Mais il y avait un autre QCM où il demandait les signes typiques…”

## 5. Successful Pedagogical Patterns

### Pattern: possible vs typical distinction

Worked because it explained why one answer can be medically possible but false in a QCM.

Technique:
- Separate “can occur” from “expected defining sign.”

### Pattern: mechanism table

Worked because it assigns each structure a role.

Technique:
- Structure → role → consequence.
