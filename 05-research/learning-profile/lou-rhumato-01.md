Learning Profile Extraction

Executive Summary

* Lou studies from past exam questions and tries to detect recurring reasoning patterns.
* She does not want isolated corrections; she wants transferable rules.
* She repeatedly asks for “global” explanations that allow her to answer new variants.
* She needs mechanisms, not just facts.
* She gets blocked when anatomy vocabulary is spatially unclear.
* She uses screenshots/QCMs as anchors for learning.
* She prefers tables, structured summaries, and “mega fiches.”
* She challenges explanations when they feel logically inconsistent.
* She distinguishes memorizing a specific answer from understanding the broader examinable concept.
* She benefits from explicit “what the professor expects” framing.
* She often asks to classify questions into reusable categories.
* She struggles with visual-spatial anatomy and movement terminology.
* She asks for contrastive explanations: “why not this?” / “what is the difference?”
* She wants high-yield revision, focused on what falls repeatedly.
* She validates learning by testing whether the explanation would work across unseen QCM variants.

1. Learning Workflow

1. Lou starts from annales or QCM screenshots.
2. She groups questions that seem to test the same underlying concept.
3. She asks for the “global” rule behind those questions.
4. When blocked, she identifies the exact contradiction or visualization problem.
5. She asks for a clearer explanation, often with concrete examples.
6. She compares related notions until the distinction becomes stable.
7. She wants the result converted into a reusable revision asset: table, mega fiche, algorithm, or summary.
8. She checks whether the asset is detailed enough to answer future variants.

Evidence:

* “ce que je veux, c’est qu’on fasse… on va commencer par ce type de question”
* “si tu comprends genre de manière globale le truc… tu peux t’en sortir”
* “fais-moi un truc un peu global, général, mais qui m’aide à bien répondre à l’entièreté des questions”
* “je ne comprends pas… c’est pas logique”
* “Bon, fais-moi un résumé un peu plus clair… sous forme d’un tableau”

4. Recurrent Difficulties

Visual-spatial anatomy

Confidence: HIGH
Evidence: “je vois pas où elle est la fracture”, “de quel côté ?”, “j’arrive pas à visualiser”
Product implication: Use annotated images, arrows, orientation conventions, and side-by-side normal/pathological comparisons.

Movement terminology

Confidence: HIGH
Evidence: confusion around flexion/extension, anterior/posterior compartments, hand vs foot logic.
Product implication: Build movement explanations from concrete gestures before anatomical terms.

Apparent contradictions

Confidence: HIGH
Evidence: “ça me semble pas logique”, “ce que je trouve assez contradictoire…”
Product implication: The assistant should explicitly separate two dimensions when concepts seem contradictory.

Mechanisms behind facts

Confidence: HIGH
Evidence: “je ne comprends pas les mécanismes”, “Pourquoi…”
Product implication: Never give isolated facts when a mechanism is involved.

Distinguishing similar concepts

Confidence: HIGH
Evidence: Garden vs Pauwels, foyer ouvert vs fermé, LCA vs anse de seau, flexion vs extension.
Product implication: Always include “do not confuse with” sections.

5. Successful Pedagogical Patterns

Separating two axes of reasoning

Worked for osteosynthesis: biological consolidation vs mechanical alignment.
Technique: split the contradiction into two independent dimensions.

“What does it normally do?”

Worked for tendon/muscle function.
Technique: function-first reasoning before pathology.

“Keyword recognition” in cases

Worked for LCA vs meniscal bucket-handle lesion.
Technique: map vignette words to diagnostic categories.

Concrete spatial language

Worked better than abstract anatomy terms.
Technique: “paume vers avant-bras,” “dos de la main,” “côté poilu/sans poils.”

6. Ineffective Pedagogical Patterns

Too-general reassurance

When told a topic was covered globally, Lou still asked whether it was enough for most questions.
Why ineffective: she needs evidence of coverage and concrete exam applicability.

Anatomical labels without spatial grounding

Terms like anterior/posterior or extension/flexion were not enough.
Why ineffective: she needed body-position references and gestures.

Correct answer without explaining why the wrong intuition fails

Lou repeatedly pushed back when an answer contradicted her mental model.
Why ineffective: she needs the conflict resolved, not bypassed.
